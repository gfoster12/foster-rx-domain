The mechanism: one receptor established, the antidepressant action hypothesisedContents
Receptor agonism and its necessity for the psychedelic state are established from primary papers; the link from receptor to antidepressant effect is hypothesised, with four competing accounts that disagree on the receptor's role; two curated pathways. 8,9,14,13,7

The figure below the first list has one job: the line between established and hypothesised. It makes no statement about the benefit of any product.

What is established, with the sources

  • Prodrug. The compound database flags psilocybin (CHEMBL194378, C12H17N2O4P, 284.25) as a prodrug with psilocin (CHEMBL65547, C12H16N2O, 204.27) as its active form; the enzymology is a 1961 paper whose title is the only quotable text 1,2,3. assessed
  • Receptor. The curated mechanism table records psilocybin as "Serotonin 2a (5-HT2a) receptor agonist" with direct interaction at human CHEMBL224 (UniProt P28223) 4,5. Of 56 curated mechanism records at that target, 52 are antagonists, 3 inverse agonists and exactly one an agonist, psilocybin; every approved drug acting at this receptor blocks it 6. The receptor maps to two curated pathways, serotonin receptors and G alpha (q) signalling 7. assessed
  • The receptor carries the psychedelic state. In healthy volunteers "the psychotomimetic effects of psilocybin were blocked dose-dependently by the serotonin-2A antagonist ketanserin" 8; in mice, head twitching after LSD "is dependent on 5-HT2A" 9; cortex-restricted re-expression of the receptor on a knockout background is sufficient for the behavioural response to hallucinogens 10. assessed

What is hypothesised, each account with its own evidence state

  • Spine growth and synaptogenesis. Serotonergic psychedelics "are capable of robustly increasing neuritogenesis and/or spinogenesis both in vitro and in vivo" 11; a single dose of psilocybin "led to ~10% increases in spine size and density" in mouse medial frontal cortex, persisting a month 12. Cells, flies, rodents. partial
  • Intracellular 5-HT2A receptors. "intracellular 5-HT2ARs mediate the plasticity-promoting properties of psychedelics; these results explain why serotonin does not engage similar plasticity mechanisms" 13. Rodent neurons and mice. partial
  • Direct binding to TrkB, independent of the receptor. LSD and psilocin "directly bind to TrkB with affinities 1,000-fold higher than those for other antidepressants"; the plasticity and antidepressant-like effects in mice "depend on TrkB binding ... but are independent of serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT2A) activation" 9. In chronically stressed mice "Neither behavioral nor electrophysiological responses to psilocybin were prevented by pretreatment with the 5-HT2A/2C antagonist ketanserin" 14. Mice. This account and the previous one contradict each other on the receptor's role, and both stand. partial
  • Network desynchronisation and relaxed priors. In healthy adults psilocybin "massively disrupted functional connectivity (FC) in cortex and subcortex, acutely causing more than threefold greater change than methylphenidate" 15; the relaxed-beliefs formulation is a theoretical synthesis and describes itself as one 16. Healthy humans; theory. partial
  • The structural biologists' own sentence. "How psychedelics mediate their actions-both therapeutic and hallucinogenic-are not understood, although activation of the 5-HT serotonin receptor (HTR2A) is key" 17. A 2023 review states the literature "is split between" receptor-dependent and receptor-independent accounts 18. assessed

  • What the methodological literature holds. A 2021 systematic review found psychedelic trials "have generally not reported pre-trial expectancy, nor the success of blinding procedures" 33. A 2024 meta-analysis of psilocybin trials rated all studies at moderate risk of bias and called for research to "delineate the influence of expectancy effects" 36.
