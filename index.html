The problem

Analysis is cheap. Trust is expensive.

You send a valuation. The other side discounts it — not because it is wrong, but because they cannot check it. That discount is priced into every negotiation you enter, and machine-generated analysis is making it worse: more output, none of it signed, none of it anyone's responsibility.

A band with conditions, and the ask marked on it

Valuation is never a point estimate. It is a range against the derived value distribution for the domain, with its conditions enumerated and the asking price marked as a negotiating position rather than a verdict.

  • Right-skewed distributions, so mean-anchored comparables overstate the median asset
  • Every gap rendered with an explanation, never filled
  • No certificate means no relation — the interface shows an em dash, never a default

Honest by construction

Every gap is rendered. None is filled.

A diligence review covers twenty-five sections. Sections with nothing behind them are shown as such, with an explanation — the stated gap is often the most valuable line in the document.

Coverage never reads as provenance. Twenty-five complete sections is not a certification, and the two are never merged.

When we are wrong, we sign the correction. A superseding certificate is issued and the original stays verifiable. An attestation you can quietly delete was never an attestation.
